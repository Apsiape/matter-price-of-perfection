\section{A gapped family with programmed entanglement}\label{sec:pairs}
Fix $b_0\geq1$, put $d=2^{b_0}$, and take a fast word $A$ from
\cite[Lemma 8.1]{gap}. The imported bottom reader selects precisely
the first $\ell(h)=\bitlength(h-1)$ interior columns at the final
tableau row. For all sufficiently large $h$, the output at its column
$j$ is $A(j)$. Indeed the first $\ell$ bits are written by time
$2^{\ell-1}-2<h-1$ in the imported timing convention. A computable
small-$g$ cutoff makes both rows of every pair interior.

Each site carries $\C^d$. Add a classical partner bit, functionally
determined by whether the lower neighbour is an active bottom-prefix
site. A two-site equality test determines it uniquely and costs two
if violated. On each active vertical pair put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pair}
 H_{\rm pair}=I-\ket{\Phi_d}\bra{\Phi_d},\qquad
 \ket{\Phi_d}=d^{-1/2}\sum_{j=0}^{d-1}\ket{j,j}.
\end{equation}
Every other quantum register, including wall registers, receives
$I-\ket0\bra0$. In a correct tableau these supports are disjoint.
All matrix entries of the Bell projection are zero or $1/d$;
the whole construction therefore has rational coefficient matrices,
fixed local dimension, and nearest-neighbour interactions. A finite
alphabet enlargement suffices for all added classical flags.
For this construction give every forbidden wall pair and every
tableau/flag violation cost two. This leaves the perfect densities
\eqref{eq:ruler} unchanged. The imported wall estimate strengthens by
exactly the number of wall violations, so a defective wall block has
margin at least $13/8$. The larger penalties will also make the
thermodynamic gap an actual eventually constant limit.

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:programmed}]
In a perfect correct block the quantum Hamiltonian has a unique
ground state, a tensor product of Bell pairs and frozen sites.
Its ground energy is zero and its gap is one. In every other classical
block all additional quantum and tableau terms remain nonnegative.
Thus no such block can undercut the imported classical lower bounds.
For $g\leq0$ the wall-free vacua freeze every register and the margin
is at least $1/16$. At fixed small $g>0$, defective wall blocks have
margin at least $13/8$, and incorrect tableau/flag blocks cost at
least two. Choose factorial volume large enough that all wrong clean
stripe pitches and the vacua have excess greater than one. This is
possible by the imported positive separation $\delta(g)$. Inside
every minimizing block the quantum spectrum is integral and has a
unit excitation. Thus $\Delta(L,g)=1$ for all sufficiently large
factorial volumes. This proves existence of the thermodynamic limit
and the stated lower bound, also at ties by excluding both minimizing
pitches when computing the separation.

Off ties, all cells have side $h=p_*(g)-1$. A single-block vector
is a product because the cell kernels are one-dimensional.
Exactly $m(h)=|A\cap[0,\ell(h))|$ Bell pairs cross the designated
bottom-prefix cut. Its reduced density matrix has $d^{m(h)}$ equal
nonzero eigenvalues, each $d^{-m(h)}$. Hence every R\'enyi entropy
in bits equals $b_0m(h)=k(h)$, and its rank is $2^{k(h)}$.
Writing $\rho_j(A)=|A\cap[0,j)|/j$, we obtain
\begin{equation}\label{eq:density}
 \frac{k(h_*(g))}{\log_2(1/g)}
       =b_0\rho_{\ell(h_*(g))}(A)+o(1).
\end{equation}
Every sufficiently large pitch, and hence every sufficiently large
prefix length, occurs. Lemma~\ref{lem:unit} gives the two density
endpoints. Choose an integer $b_0\geq\max(1,b)$ and the imported fast
word with endpoints $a/b_0,b/b_0$. This proves attainment. Equal
endpoints are precisely the nonnegative $\Dtwo$ targets by the imported
density theorem. Zero is included, for example by an eventually empty
word. Local dimension and the finite program may depend on the target.
\end{proof}

The union of all designated bottom prefixes has entropy $k(h)$ per
cell, or $k(h)/p^2$ per lattice site in bits. Whole-cell unions have
zero entropy in these states. A label-free variant uses the maximum
entropy of a horizontal segment of length $s(g)$ satisfying
$\ell(h)\leq s(g)\leq p/2$. Once $s(g)+\ell(h)<p$, such a segment
can meet at most one active prefix, and a segment covering that prefix
attains exactly $k(h)$. Thus the maximum equals the cell entropy;
a factor-of-two comparison would not suffice to identify this
logarithmic coefficient.

At a fixed finite commensurate volume, approaching a classical pitch
crossing from one side produces an arbitrarily small positive competing
block splitting. This explains why the theorem uses a fixed-$g$
thermodynamic distinct-level gap. It supplies no identification with
every infinite-volume GNS gap and no conventional continuous critical
point. In the quantum pair layer, connected correlations vanish unless
the supports touch a common pair; a diverging structural pitch is
compatible with this observation.

\begin{corollary}[Exact arithmetic classes in an effective cell class]\label{cor:classes}
For uniquely grounded selected cells with uniformly effective algebraic
finite matrices and a computably specified cell-anchored region, finite
lower, upper, and existing entropy or log-Schmidt-rank coefficients
belong to $\LS,\LP,\Dtwo$, respectively. Together with
Theorem~\ref{thm:programmed}, these classes are exact in this class.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Exact real algebra computes the simple ground eigenvector and its
reduced algebraic matrix. Its exact rank is decidable. Its eigenvalues
are computable and $-x\log_2x$, extended continuously by zero, computes
the entropy. The normalized data therefore have uniform rational
approximants. Apply Proposition~\ref{prop:transfer} and
Lemma~\ref{lem:unit}; finite endpoints give the eventual boundedness
needed in the ratio comparison. No convergence modulus is asserted.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Cell perturbations and content energy}
\begin{proposition}[Perturbed pair cells]\label{prop:pairfields}
Add Hermitian fields $v,v'$ of norm at most $\eta$ at the two endpoints
of each pair. For $\eta<1/4$ every pair has a unique ground state and
gap at least $1-4\eta$. The cell still factors into pair and single-site
blocks. Arbitrary pair fields preserve full Schmidt rank whenever
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rankradius}
 \eta<\frac1{4+2\sqrt d}.
\end{equation}
Mirror fields $v'=v^{\mathsf T}$ preserve full rank throughout
$\eta<1/4$. If the same specified pair fields are repeated, writing
their pair entropy as $s(v,v')$ gives exactly
$S(R_c)=m(h)s(v,v')$; for site-dependent fields it is the sum of the
actual pair entropies.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The total pair perturbation has norm at most $2\eta$. Weyl's
inequalities separate the former zero eigenvalue from the rest by
$1-4\eta$. Frozen single-site blocks have gap at least $1-2\eta$.
Disjointness gives the cell factorization.

Write a pair ground vector as $a\Phi_d+b$, with $b\perp\Phi_d$,
and write its energy as $E\leq2\eta$. With
$Q_0=I-\ket{\Phi_d}\bra{\Phi_d}$, its eigenvalue equation gives
\[
 [(1-E)I+Q_0VQ_0]b=-aQ_0V\Phi_d,\qquad
 \norm b\leq\frac{2\eta}{1-4\eta}|a|.
\]
The coefficient matrix is $aI/\sqrt d+B$, with
$\norm B_{\rm op}\leq\norm b$. It is invertible under
\eqref{eq:rankradius}. In particular $a\ne0$, since the displayed
inequality otherwise forces the vector to vanish.

For mirror fields, vectorization makes the local field action
$X\mapsto vX+Xv$. In an eigenbasis of $v$, all off-diagonal entries
have energy at least $1-2\eta>E$. On the diagonal subspace the
rank-one Bell term yields
\[
 X\ \text{proportional to}\ [(1-E)I+2v]^{-1}.
\]
All denominators are at least $1-4\eta>0$, so every Schmidt
coefficient is nonzero. Tensor products then multiply ranks and add
the actual block entropies. Finite-dimensional continuity gives
$s(v,v')\to\log_2d$ as the fields vanish. Computable algebraic repeated
fields give a computable positive $s$ in the stated radii.
\end{proof}

The entropy factor is not determined by the norm bound. For $d=2$,
equal scalar fields leave the Bell entropy one, whereas fields
$\eta Z$ on both endpoints give the ground vector of
\[
 \begin{pmatrix}1/2+2\eta&-1/2\\-1/2&1/2-2\eta\end{pmatrix},
\]
with entropy less than one for $\eta>0$. Repeated specified fields
rescale the cell entropy coefficient by $s/b_0$, while leaving its
rank coefficient unchanged within the proved radius. This is a
cell-level statement. Their energy shift can alter classical selection.

\begin{proposition}[A design-dependent energetic competitor]\label{prop:competitor}
Suppose a specified legal design admits a defect costing at most $C$
which changes $m(h)$ independently priced content units by the
favourable sign, without any other energy change. A field charging
those units at magnitude $|\delta|$ makes this competitor favourable
whenever $|\delta|m(h)>C$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Subtract the two expectation energies. The difference is at most
$C-|\delta|m(h)$.
\end{proof}
An actual bounded-cost defect must be exhibited before applying this
criterion. It is not a universal instability theorem. A logarithmically
growing content cost does not satisfy the $h^{-3}$ hypothesis of
Lemma~\ref{lem:shifted}; no material stability window follows from the
pair calculation alone.
