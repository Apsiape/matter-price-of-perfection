\section{Main statements and conventions}\label{sec:main}
A finite nonlocal game can demand unbounded entanglement as its loss tends
to zero. We ask how that demand can enter a Hamiltonian. There are two
distinct statements. An explicit lattice construction programs a
ground-state entanglement coefficient directly. A referee cell instead
certifies entanglement through a game's precision price. The first is
an unconditional construction using an imported ruler; the second is an
unconditional theorem about an ideal legal history space. Their
composition into a particular game-driven translation-invariant material
has additional, explicitly stated hypotheses.

Entropies and distillation rates are in bits. Natural logarithms are
written $\ln$. For a finite game $G$, $D_G(\eps)$ is the least common
local Hilbert-space dimension of a finite strategy of loss at most
$\eps$, allowing mixed shared states. The usable price is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:price}
 \price(\eps)=\inf\{\ED(\rho):\text{a finite strategy on $\rho$ has loss at most $\eps$}\}.
\end{equation}
An empty infimum is $+\infty$. Whenever an energy bound gives a loss
larger than one, the resulting price statement is understood simply as
the trivial bound at loss one. We use no precision asymptotic there.
These conventions are those of \cite{precision,unprice}; in particular,
the infimum is over the original mixed strategy state.

The distinct-level gap of a nonscalar finite Hamiltonian is
$\Delta(L,g)=E_1^{\rm distinct}-E_0$: the whole ground eigenspace is
removed. Write
\[
 \Delta_\infty(g)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\Delta(n!,g)
\]
when the limit exists. The volume limit always precedes $g\downarrow0$.
A classical block fixes every wall and tableau label. Our designated
cell entropies are evaluated in one minimizing classical block, off the
pitch ties. Degenerate quantum cell spaces require a further state
choice; they never imply that all ground vectors factor over cells.

\begin{theorem}[Programmed entanglement with a positive gap]\label{thm:programmed}
Let $0\leq a\leq b<\infty$, with $a\in\LS$ and $b\in\LP$.
There is a fixed finite-local-dimension, rational, translation-invariant
onsite and nearest-neighbour family on $\Z^2$, affine in $g$, such that
\[
 \Delta(L,g)\geq1/16\quad(g\leq0),\qquad
 \Delta_\infty(g)\geq5/8\quad(0<g<\bar g).
\]
The second assertion includes pitch ties. Off ties, the cell-anchored
bottom-prefix region $R_c(g)$ has, in every single-classical-block ground
state,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:programmed}
 S(R_c)=\log_2\SR(R_c:R_c^c)=k(h),\quad
 k(h)=b_0|A\cap[0,\ell(h))|,\quad
 \ell(h)=\bitlength(h-1),
\end{equation}
where $b_0$ is a positive integer and $h=p_*(g)-1$.
The lower and upper limits of $S(R_c)/\log_2(1/g)$ are $(a,b)$.
Consequently every nonnegative $\Dtwo$ real is an existing coefficient;
when it equals $\kappa$, $\SR(R_c:R_c^c)=g^{-\kappa+o(1)}$.
\end{theorem}

This is a bound on the fixed-$g$ thermodynamic gap, not a positive
finite-volume bound uniform in $g$ near every level crossing. The region
grows within a cell and has boundary length $O(\log(1/g))$. The entropy
does not violate an area law. The pitch diverges, but it is not asserted
to be a connected-correlation length. The proof is in
Section~\ref{sec:pairs}.

For the second main theorem let $n=T+1$, $M=n^k$, and let $L_k$ be the
unnormalized combinatorial Laplacian on $\{0,\ldots,T\}^k$.
Its edge quadratic form is $\sum_{\{v,w\}}\norm{\psi_v-\psi_w}^2$.
Assume an exact local rewinding $W=W_A\otimes W_B$ gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cell}
 W^*HW=L_k\otimes I+\ket{0^k}\bra{0^k}\otimes(I-P)
                    +\ket{T^k}\bra{T^k}\otimes Q.
\end{equation}
Here $P=\ket{\pi,0}\bra{\pi,0}\otimes I_{\rm sys}$ initializes
coherent questions and local work, and $Q$ is the final losing
projection conjugated by the two honest local, question-controlled
circuits. Section~\ref{sec:referee} specifies this assumption fully.
Let $G_k=(L_k^+)_{0^k,0^k}$ and let $\mu_1$ be its first positive
eigenvalue.

\begin{theorem}[Referee extraction and resistance]\label{thm:extraction}
For every unit legal vector $\Psi$ of energy $\lambda$, a finite
strategy on $W^*\Psi$ has loss at most $C_k M\lambda$, with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:constant}
 C_k=2(3+4\sqrt{G_k})^2+\frac{2}{M\mu_1}.
\end{equation}
It retains exactly the Schmidt coefficients of $\Psi$. Thus
\[
 \SR(\Psi)\geq D_G(C_kM\lambda),\qquad
 S_A(\Psi)\geq\price(C_kM\lambda).
\]
For every legal density matrix $\rho$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mixed-main}
 \ED(\rho)\geq\price(C_kM\tr(\rho H)).
\end{equation}
The resistance has the asymptotics
\[
 G_1\sim T/3,\qquad G_2=(2/\pi)\ln T+O(1),\qquad G_3<14.
\]
For $k\geq2$, $2/(M\mu_1)\leq1/2$; in particular three clocks admit
the absolute extraction constant $C_3<647$. Two clocks admit the
alternative elementary constant $5(1+\sqrt{2T})^2$.
\end{theorem}

The resistance is for injection at a corner and uniform withdrawal,
namely $(\delta_c-M^{-1}\one)^*L_k^+(\delta_c-M^{-1}\one)$.
It is not obtained by shorting all other vertices together. A third
idle clock improves the extraction coefficient while increasing the
referee-volume dilution $M$. Section~\ref{sec:referee} proves the
theorem and the logarithmic obstruction. Sections~\ref{sec:conditional}
and \ref{sec:thermal} give, respectively, the conditional material
composition and necessary physical resource bounds.

\subsection{Imported results}\label{sec:imports}
We use the released version 2.0.0 of \emph{Uncomputable critical
exponents} \cite{gap}. Its Lemmas 2.1--2.2 and Corollary 2.3 give the
five-symbol ruler, its charging estimate, and selection/separation on
factorial tori. Section 5 gives the functional nearest-neighbour tableau
with the bottom-prefix reader, and Section 11 anchors it inside ruler
cells. Lemma 8.1 supplies a computable binary word with any admissible
density endpoints, with each bit computable in time polynomial in its
index's bitlength. Proposition 10.1 is the limit calculus. Theorem 1.1
of that paper is the earlier gap-exponent construction; none of its
counter-spectrum argument is needed for our Bell-pair layer.

From \emph{The uncomputable price of precision}, version 4.0.0
\cite{unprice}, we use Theorem 1.1 for the matched dimension and
original-state usable price, Theorem 1.2 for the halting game,
Theorem 7.1 for its explicit finite models, Theorem 3.1 for its fixed
polynomial-to-Boolean compiler, and Theorem 12.3 for its original-state
usable coefficient. In particular
\begin{equation}\label{eq:halting-import}
\begin{gathered}
 \alpha_K=\frac{3+\theta_K}{4},\qquad
 \theta_K=\sum_{e\in K}4^{-(e+1)},\\
 \log_2D_{G_K}(\eps)=\alpha_K\log_2(1/\eps)+o(\log(1/\eps)),\\
 d_{G_K}^{\to}(\eps)=\alpha_K\log_2(1/\eps)+o(\log(1/\eps)).
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
For even $N$, the finite model has
\begin{equation}\label{eq:finite-import}
 K_N=\{e<N/2:h(e)<2^N\},\quad
 \theta_N=\sum_{e\in K_N}4^{-(e+1)},\quad
 d_N=2^{3N+k_N},\quad k_N=\theta_NN+O(\log(N+2)),
\end{equation}
with defining operator defects at most $3\cdot4^{-N}$ and
$\theta_N\uparrow\theta_K$.
The compiler and the modification in Section~\ref{sec:circuits} give
strategies of system dimension $c_0d_N$ and loss at most
$\eps_N=C_1 16^{-N}$, with fixed constants.
The Boolean correspondence is imported from \emph{The price of
precision}, version 1.1.0, Theorem 4.9 \cite{precision}. That paper's
Theorem 11.1 already gives matching pure Schmidt-rank, pure-entropy,
and mixed-state formation coefficients for every positive rational.
We do not re-prove these game or ruler theorems.

\subsection{Relation to earlier work}\label{sec:prior}
Computational lattice constructions begin here with the gap
undecidability of Cubitt, P\'erez-Garc\'ia and Wolf \cite{cpw}; marker
segments and undecidable entanglement behavior occur in
\cite{bclp}, size-sensitive phase changes in \cite{size}, and
continuous-parameter checkerboard/marker constructions in
Bausch, Cubitt and Watson \cite{bcw}. Purcell, Li and Cubitt
\cite{chaitin} place a transition at a halting probability. Undecidable
renormalisation destinations \cite{rg} and complexity of critical
locations \cite{criticalpoints} concern different asymptotic questions.
Schmidhuber's algorithmic ensemble \cite{schmidhuber} has computable
critical inverse temperature $\ln2$ and a partition function equal to
a halting probability there. Public bootstrap-percolation papers
\cite{bootstrap,droplet} announce algorithmic exponent
uncomputability across update families. We make no claim to the first
uncomputable lattice exponent.

Arithmetic limit classifications \cite{zheng} and density-pair
realization \cite{density} are mathematical inputs. Symbolic growth
invariants and entropy classifications already encode noncomputability
in local rules \cite{meyerovitch,hochman}. Commensurate staircases
\cite{bak,fisher,aubry} and wall-density transitions \cite{pokrovsky}
also precede the present construction. Finite analytic spectral branches
are governed by Rellich's theorem as presented by Kato \cite{kato}.
Our new realization concerns a programmed logarithmic coefficient for
a specified growing cut in a distinct-level-gapped family.
Unusual chain entanglement laws \cite{movassagh,zhang,fredkin} and
standard pure-state entanglement concentration \cite{bbps} are separate
precedents; pair counting itself is elementary.

Feynman--Kitaev computation \cite{feynman,ksv}, local histories
\cite{adkllr,agik,gi}, multiple clocks \cite{breuckmann}, modified and
graph clocks \cite{bCrosson}, idling and endpoint analysis
\cite{caha}, and nonunitary histories \cite{usher} are established.
Classical network resistance \cite{cserti,doyle} and resistance-based
quantum witnesses \cite{belovs} are also established. The contribution
here is their quantitative use in fresh-question extraction with
unchanged Schmidt data and an explicit game-price certificate.
Precision-dependent dimension witnesses already occur in
Slofstra--Vidick \cite{sv}; the matched prices used here are the
imports just stated.

The quasi-local approximation argument below is standard in the spirit
of Scholz--Werner \cite{scholz}; tensor/commuting separation is supplied
by \cite{mip}. Thermal information \cite{wolf}, entangling rates
\cite{bravyi,van}, squashed entanglement \cite{cw}, and correlation
work costs \cite{huber} are prior inequalities. Our resource bounds
substitute the imported price into these inequalities. Certified
equilibrium algorithms \cite{fawzi} do not imply computability of an
arbitrary limiting coupling coefficient.

Instability already has direct precedents in Castilla-Castellano--Lucia
\cite{instability} and the October version of Xu et al. \cite{xu}; their
gap conventions must be kept distinct from ours. Rotationally symmetric
gap undecidability \cite{rotation} is another separate result.
Strict-boundary stability \cite{miekisz,miekisznew}, robust computation
in tilings \cite{durand}, and low-temperature quasicrystalline Gibbs
states \cite{taati} do not supply the selection and interface rules
required by our conditional composition. We assert neither an open
interaction-ball stability theorem nor a general local-noise law.
