\section{Temperature, preparation time, and work}\label{sec:thermal}
The mixed-state certificate \eqref{eq:mixed-main} applies to any legal
state, including a Gibbs state when the legal space is a genuine
bipartite tensor product. Its composition with established physical
inequalities gives necessary resource bounds. We set $k_B=\hbar=1$.
We use \cite{cw}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mi-entanglement}
 \ED(\rho)\leq E_D(\rho)\leq E_{\rm sq}(\rho)\leq I(A:B)_\rho/2.
\end{equation}
The last inequality uses the trivial extension in the definition of
squashed entanglement. All quantities in \eqref{eq:mi-entanglement}
are in bits.

\begin{theorem}[Necessary thermal, temporal, and work budgets]\label{thm:resources}
Suppose a finite bipartite state plays $G$ at loss $\ell$.
For the Gibbs state of $H=H_A+H_B+H_\partial$ at inverse temperature
$\beta$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:temperature}
 \beta\norm{H_\partial}\geq(\ln2)\price(\ell).
\end{equation}
For unitary preparation from a product state, with time-dependent cut
interaction on fixed interacting dimensions, let $d_\partial$ be
the smaller interacting local dimension. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:time}
 18\int_0^t\norm{H_\partial(s)}\log_2d_\partial\,ds
                       \geq\price(\ell).
\end{equation}
For average-work isothermal preparation at temperature $\Theta$ from
$\gamma_A\otimes\gamma_B$, with the noninteracting Hamiltonian
$H_A+H_B$ restored at the endpoint and no uncharged resource,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:work}
 \mathcal W\geq2\Theta\ln2\,\price(\ell).
\end{equation}
If $\price(\ell)=\alpha_G\log_2(1/\ell)+o(\log(1/\ell))$,
the same imported coefficient appears in the right sides. These are
lower bounds; no matching preparation protocol is asserted.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For the Gibbs state $\rho$, put $\sigma=\rho_A\otimes\rho_B$.
Free-energy minimality gives
\[
 \beta^{-1}I_{\rm nats}(A:B)_\rho
 \leq\tr[H(\sigma-\rho)]
 =\tr[H_\partial(\sigma-\rho)]\leq2\norm{H_\partial}.
\]
The equality cancels local Hamiltonian terms using equal marginals.
This is the thermal mutual-information bound of \cite{wolf}.
Equation~\eqref{eq:mi-entanglement} and
$\ED(\rho)\geq\price(\ell)$ prove \eqref{eq:temperature}, with
the factor $\ln2$ converting bits to nats.

For pure product input, the small incremental entangling theorem
\cite{van}, with constant $18$, bounds the entropy derivative by
$18\norm{H_\partial(s)}\log_2d_\partial$, independent of spectator
dimensions. Integrate from zero initial entropy. For mixed product
input, purify each party locally, evolve with those purifying registers
as spectators, and use monotonicity under local discarding. This bounds
the final distillable entanglement by the same integrated entropy,
and gives \eqref{eq:time}. If $\norm{H_\partial}\leq J$, it yields
$t\geq\price(\ell)/(18J\log_2d_\partial)$. Pre-existing shared
entanglement or an increasing interface is outside this hypothesis.

For work, the stipulated free-energy accounting gives
\[
 \mathcal W\geq F(\rho)-F(\gamma_A\otimes\gamma_B)
       =\Theta D(\rho\Vert\gamma_A\otimes\gamma_B),
\]
with natural relative entropy. Its exact decomposition is
\begin{align*}
 D(\rho\Vert\gamma_A\otimes\gamma_B)
 &=I_{\rm nats}(A:B)_\rho
   +D(\rho_A\Vert\gamma_A)+D(\rho_B\Vert\gamma_B)\\
 &\geq I_{\rm nats}(A:B)_\rho.
\end{align*}
Apply \eqref{eq:mi-entanglement}. This is the standard correlation
work bound \cite{huber}, with the game's usable demand inserted.
\end{proof}

Subtracting a scalar from the cut interaction can improve its norm
without changing the argument. The bounds apply to the true physical
tensor product; a nonproduct conditioning on legality cannot silently
replace that state space. The work statement is not a general equality
between creation work and any local-reset heat convention.

\begin{corollary}[Fixed-temperature and fixed-time coefficients]\label{cor:fixedresources}
For Gibbs states with a uniform cut norm $\norm{H_\partial}\leq J$,
every nonnegative entanglement quantity $E\leq I/2$ satisfies
\[
 0\leq E\leq\beta J/\ln2.
\]
At fixed positive temperature its coefficient after division by
$\log_2(1/g)$ is zero. The same conclusion holds at fixed preparation
time from product input when the integrated interface rate is uniformly
bounded. For a two-corner referee with $J\leq2$, the thermal bound is
$E\leq2\beta/\ln2$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The first bound is the Gibbs mutual-information inequality. For
preparation, local purifications give
$I(A:B)/2\leq S(A\,\text{with local purifier})$, since mutual
information decreases under local discarding. The integrated rate
therefore bounds every $E\leq I/2$. Divide either uniform bound by
the divergent logarithm.
\end{proof}
No gap assumption is needed here. Thermal marginal entropy includes
local thermal entropy and is not covered. Nor are all possible mixed
entanglement measures, growing interfaces, or fluctuating-wall Gibbs
states without the specified cut and state convention. An uncomputable
coefficient may appear in these necessary precision inequalities
without being a proved optimal resource rate or the only observable
that retains it.

\subsection{A finite-dimensional Gibbs energy estimate}
For any finite legal Gibbs Hamiltonian of dimension $D$ and minimum
energy $e_0$, free-energy minimality against a pure ground state gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gibbsenergy}
 \tr(\rho_\Theta H)\leq e_0+\Theta\ln D.
\end{equation}
Thus the extraction certificate has effective precision at most
$C'[\eps_N+M\Theta\ln D]$ when $Me_0\leq\eps_N$.
To retain a depth-$N$ leading coefficient in this lower certificate it
suffices that
\[
 -\log_2(C'M\Theta\ln D)\geq4N-o(N)
\]
and $\log_2C'=o(N)$, or more restrictively
$M\Theta\ln D=O(\eps_N)$. Smallness relative only to an inverse
polynomial of depth does not imply this exponential precision. No
claim of a thermal material region follows without controlling the
full physical dimension, illegal sectors, and wall distribution.

\subsection{Thermal pair correlations}\label{sec:thermalpairs}
The Bell-pair cell provides an exact comparison at the level of mutual
information. For a pair \eqref{eq:pair}, let $d=2^{b_0}$ and
\[
 \rho_\beta=\frac{I+(e^\beta-1)\ket{\Phi_d}\bra{\Phi_d}}
                         {Z},\qquad Z=e^\beta+d^2-1,\qquad F=e^\beta/Z.
\]
Both marginals are $I/d$. The mutual information in bits is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pairmi}
 i_{b_0}(\beta)=2\log_2d+F\log_2F
                +(1-F)\log_2\frac{1-F}{d^2-1}.
\end{equation}
It is computable for computable $\beta$, positive for every $\beta>0$,
and tends to $2b_0$ as $\beta\to\infty$. Positivity follows because
equality with the product of the maximally mixed marginals occurs only
at $\beta=0$. Independent disjoint pairs therefore give
$I(R_c:R_c^c)=m(h)i_{b_0}(\beta)$ at every fixed positive inverse
temperature. The normalized half-mutual-information coefficient is
the programmed coefficient times $i_{b_0}(\beta)/(2b_0)$.

This is not all-temperature entanglement survival. These isotropic
states are separable exactly when
\begin{equation}\label{eq:separable}
 F\leq1/d\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad e^\beta\leq d+1.
\end{equation}
For the separable direction, twirl product states by
$U\otimes\overline U$; their Bell overlap ranges over $[0,1/d]$
and determines the isotropic state. For the other direction, partial
transpose has an antisymmetric eigenvalue
$[1-(e^\beta-1)/d]/Z<0$. This is consistent with the isotropic-state
criterion in \cite{horodecki}. In particular, for
$\Theta\geq1/\ln(d+1)$, every pair and their product are separable
despite nonzero mutual information. The comparison concerns total
correlations. Its cut norm grows with the number of pairs and hence
does not satisfy the bounded-interface hypothesis of
Corollary~\ref{cor:fixedresources}.
